## Lifespan/fecundity extension (Durham 2014 + Huang 2020, 6 elastic net LOO-CV runs)

All runs use `scripts/run_dgrpool_phenotype.py` with default `--sex F`/`--spectral-sex F` (same-sex comparison; no cross-sex flag needed, unlike Unckless). "Collapsed" uses the same 0.2 prediction-SD/true-SD threshold as elsewhere.

| Phenotype | Study | n lines | CV R² | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lifespan (mated) | Durham 2014 | 96 | −0.085 | Genuine null: predictions collapse to the training mean |
| Fecundity Week 1 | Durham 2014 | 96 | −0.058 | Genuine null: predictions collapse to the training mean |
| Fecundity Week 7 | Durham 2014 | 76 | −0.006 | Genuine null: predictions do not collapse, but carry no meaningful predictive power |
| Lifespan 18°C (mated) | Huang 2020 | 99 | −0.023 | Genuine null: predictions collapse to the training mean |
| **Lifespan 25°C (mated)** | **Huang 2020** | **102** | **+0.056** | Not a mean-collapse artefact (prediction SD/true SD ratio = 0.294), but weak and uncorrected for multiple comparisons: a candidate for follow-up, not a standalone finding |
| Lifespan 28°C (mated) | Huang 2020 | 99 | −0.034 | Genuine null: predictions collapse to the training mean |

**Interpretation: lifespan across mating status and temperature.** Including the earlier Ivanov 2015 result (virgin females, R²=−0.052), five independent lifespan tests have now been run: 1 virgin-female cohort (Ivanov) and 4 mated-female cohorts (Durham, and Huang at 18/25/28°C). Four of the five collapse to the training mean with no usable signal. **Lifespan shows no reliable FTIR signal regardless of mating status.** The mated-vs-virgin distinction that motivated this extension does not explain the nulls. The one exception, Huang 25°C (R²=+0.056), is weak and not corrected for multiple comparisons, but it's worth noting that 25°C is the standard DGRP rearing temperature, which is a more defensible observation than a general claim about mating status, since it points to a specific, non-arbitrary condition rather than an untested hypothesis about reproductive state. Treated the same way as the Unckless Protein/low-glucose result above: a candidate for follow-up, not a finding. (The two Durham fecundity results, week 1 and week 7, are a separate phenotype and also both null; see table above.)

## EATRIS-Plus human lipidomics-to-BMI (different species, different pipeline)

Third and final task from the same email that produced the Durham/Huang extension above: whether lipidome features predict BMI quantitatively, tested directly in humans rather than by analogy from the DGRP fly panel. **This is not a DGRP/FTIR phenotype file** and does not fit the table format above: different species (human, not *Drosophila*), different profiling technology (LC-MS lipidomics, not FTIR), different pipeline (`scripts/run_eatris_lipidomics_bmi.py`, standalone, not `run_dgrpool_phenotype.py`), so it is documented separately rather than as a row in the main table, and is **not** included in the DGRP cross-phenotype tally at the end of the Lifespan/fecundity section above.

**Source:** EATRIS-Plus multi-omics cohort (Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.17514796), 125 healthy human adults, an R `MultiAssayExperiment` object with 15 omics experiments. Raw files at `phenotype-data/raw/mae_mae.rds` (tracked) and `phenotype-data/raw/mae_experiments.h5` (104MB, gitignored since it's over GitHub's 100MB limit; download separately per `REPRODUCE.md` §9). Structure established by `scripts/explore_eatris_mae.R` (inspection only): BMI is complete (125/125, range 21-37) with zero sample-ID mismatches across all 15 experiments.

**Extraction:** `scripts/prepare_eatris_lipidomics.R` pulls two lipidomics assays, positive mode (196 features) and negative mode (164 features), both with full 125/125 sample overlap, plus a combined 360-feature matrix (`pos_`/`neg_` prefixed columns), and the matched BMI phenotype vector. Assay data is HDF5-backed with a working-directory-dependent path baked into the `.rds`; the script resolves this via an absolute path computed with `here()` rather than a fragile `setwd()`. Output files: `EATRIS_Lipidomics_positive.tsv`, `EATRIS_Lipidomics_negative.tsv`, `EATRIS_Lipidomics_combined.tsv`, `EATRIS_BMI.tsv`, `EATRIS_covariates.tsv` (Sex/Age/BMI, used only for the confound check below).

**Results** (elastic net LOO-CV, best of 4 methods tested: PLS, Ridge, LASSO, elastic net, same per-fold `StandardScaler`/hyperparameter-selection discipline as the DGRP regularised-regression scripts):

| Condition | n | p | CV R² | Spearman ρ | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Positive mode | 125 | 196 | **+0.283** | +0.545 | Real, non-collapsed signal |
| Negative mode | 125 | 164 | +0.219 | +0.526 | Real, non-collapsed signal |
| Combined | 125 | 360 | +0.271 | +0.546 | Real, non-collapsed signal |

Unlike almost every DGRP result above, none of these collapse: all three conditions, across all four methods, produce real, non-degenerate CV R² (0.10-0.28 range). Positive mode alone slightly outperforms the combined matrix; both clearly beat negative mode alone.

**Verification before accepting this as real.** (1) Per-fold fitting discipline confirmed directly from the code, matching the DGRP-script standard, with one inherited (not new) caveat: PLS's optimal `n_components` is chosen post-hoc by comparing already-computed LOO-CV scores, a practice carried over from `run_pls_analysis.py` that does not affect the elastic net result. (2) Sex/Age confound check (`EATRIS_covariates.tsv`): Sex-BMI r=+0.291 (modest), Age-BMI r=-0.011 (negligible); a trivial Sex+Age-only baseline achieves R²=+0.039. The lipidomics models beat this 5-7x, ruling out a demographic-proxy explanation. (3) Literature context: a large, externally-validated lipidomics-BMI study (FINRISK/MDC-CC, n=1,061+250, PLOS Biology 2019) reports BMI R²=0.47 with a far more favourable feature-to-sample ratio after LASSO selection than used here; this exact cohort's own companion paper (bioRxiv 2024.11.07.622407) found lipidomics alone to be a weaker single-omics BMI predictor than metabolites, with multi-omics integration beating any single layer, directly consistent with the modest, non-dominant signal found here.

**Verdict.** A real, non-collapsed signal clearly distinguishable from a trivial demographic baseline, categorically different from every DGRP result in this project. But it is modest relative to the well-powered external literature ceiling, obtained without external validation in a small-sample, high-dimensional (p>n) regime, and consistent with (not better than) what this cohort's own authors found for single-omics BMI prediction. The first genuinely positive, thoroughly-vetted result outside the DGRP starvation-resistance work in this entire project.
